**Supplementary Figure S1 — ChromData format versions, access by layout and by format, and round trips (Takei 2025 cerebellum whole-cell subsets, 62 per-spot tracks).** **a**, File size and **b**, full-read time into memory vs spots (median of 3 fresh processes; same data and protocol as Fig. 2c). Flat Parquet (the spot table with coordinates and tracks, pyarrow defaults) is the storage engine under chromdata.zarr, so it is the lower bound without hierarchy, index or metadata; the difference to chromdata.zarr is the cost of those. chromdata.zarr 2.2 (the current format: coordinates stored once per chromosome, cell-sorted primary tables, spot columns derived per bin / cell / trace) is 14 % smaller than flat Parquet at 1e7 spots (1.81 vs 2.11 GB) and reads 25 % faster (0.57 vs 0.76 s; peak memory 9.1 vs 12.8 GB); 2.1 (every spot table partitioned by chromosome) and 2.0 (cell-sorted) are the previous layouts; float32 coordinates halve the coordinate columns of 2.0; h5cd 2.0 / 1.x are the HDF5 containers (still read). **c**, Backed `get_chrom` and **d**, backed `get_cell` by chromdata.zarr layout (median of 20 random ids × 3 fresh processes; open markers: 1e8 spots from replicated cells): all columns (solid) and coordinates only (dashed; 2.1 and 2.2). 2.2 keeps 2.1's fast coordinate-only `get_chrom` and 2.0's fast `get_cell`, but `get_chrom` with all tracks reads a slice of every cell-sorted row group, like 2.0 (0.52 s at 1e7, 6.7 s at 1e8; 2.1: 0.04 / 0.49 s). **e**, Round trips: what survives an export followed by an import through U-Chrom's readers and writers (`benchmarks/fig2/f_roundtrip.py`; values compared exactly, coordinates to the format's precision): filled, preserved; half-filled, partial, or not representable in that format; open, not applicable. PDB is export-only and .3dg import-only in U-Chrom. This tests U-Chrom's converters (for example, `cd.to_anndata()` exports the cell table, not the spot coordinates); what each container can represent natively is Fig. 2b. **f**, One trace and **g**, one chromosome across formats: the time to open (backed chromdata.zarr) or load (FOF-CT CSV, AnnData `.h5ad`) or lazily open (SpatialData) the file, then get the trace or chromosome (same protocol as Fig. 2d, which shows one cell). At 1e7 spots, one trace: chromdata.zarr 42 ms, SpatialData 0.74 s, AnnData 7.6 s; one chromosome: chromdata.zarr 0.56 s with all 62 tracks and 49 ms with coordinates only, SpatialData 0.89 s, AnnData 7.8 s. FOF-CT stops at 1e6 spots (8.4 s) because loading the 1e7-spot CSV exceeds our 40 GB peak-memory safety limit. Data: `paper/fig2/results/b_storage.json`, `c_access.json`, `f_roundtrip.json`, `cd22_v2_variants.json` (all variants and ablations); machine as in Fig. 2.
