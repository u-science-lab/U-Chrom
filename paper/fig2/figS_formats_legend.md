**Supplementary Figure — ChromData format versions and the flat Parquet reference (Takei 2025 cerebellum whole-cell subsets, 62 per-spot tracks).** **a**, File size and **b**, full-read time into memory vs spots (median of 3 fresh processes; same data and protocol as Fig. 2c). Flat Parquet (the spot table with coordinates and tracks, pyarrow defaults) is the storage engine under chromdata.zarr, so it is the lower bound without hierarchy, index or metadata; the difference to chromdata.zarr is the cost of those. chromdata.zarr 2.2 (the current format: coordinates stored once per chromosome, cell-sorted primary tables, spot columns derived per bin / cell / trace) is 14 % smaller than flat Parquet at 1e7 spots (1.81 vs 2.11 GB) and reads 25 % faster (0.57 vs 0.76 s; peak memory 9.1 vs 12.8 GB); 2.1 (every spot table partitioned by chromosome) and 2.0 (cell-sorted) are the previous layouts; float32 coordinates halve the coordinate columns of 2.0; h5cd 2.0 / 1.x are the HDF5 containers (still read). **c**, Backed `get_chrom` and **d**, backed `get_cell` by chromdata.zarr layout (median of 20 random ids × 3 fresh processes; open markers: 1e8 spots from replicated cells): all columns (solid) and coordinates only (dashed; 2.1 and 2.2). 2.2 keeps 2.1's fast coordinate-only `get_chrom` and 2.0's fast `get_cell`, but `get_chrom` with all tracks reads a slice of every cell-sorted row group, like 2.0. Data: `paper/fig2/results/b_storage.json`, `c_access.json`, `cd22_v2_variants.json` (all variants and ablations); machine as in Fig. 2.
